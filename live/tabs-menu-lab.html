How to read this page

One bar, one strip of logos, one set of candidates at a time. The strip is the live one to the pixel, cell for cell, and no candidate is allowed to move it. The console on the right switches the tabs and the menu on their own axes, and the pairs worth seeing whole are commands in it. Point at a menu item and the selected logo is in the same glance: that is the whole question, and a pretty hover on its own does not answer it.

The law of the line is not settled. A rule can mean the open menu and the selected tab at once: Cartier does exactly that, underlining Jewellery in the bar and Rings inside the panel on one screen. What separates them there is hierarchy, nearness to what they control and how long the state lives, not thickness. So the pair to look at hardest is T3 with H4, two rules on one composition, and the question to answer while looking at it is whether the strip still says which brand is chosen while the menu is open. T1 was the answer the recon proposed to that question and it is no longer standing: a rule that hugs the mark takes the accidental width of a brand and turns into a stump under a compact logo such as Amex, so the strip keeps the rule under the cell, exactly as it runs on the live page today.

Three things the rig found while this was built. H3 answers with a panel that covers the strip, so while the menu is open there is no telling which brand is chosen: on the real home page the hero stands between the two, here they are deliberately one under the other. T2 depends on which brand is chosen, because the row is not even in optical weight: Samsung and Christie's are heavy marks and Workday and Spotify are light ones, so the quietest brand makes the quietest selection. And below 1280 the bar folds into a burger, so all four menu candidates are a desktop question and nothing else.

What is not on this page. Dior's white plate on a grey track is a real luxury tablist, and it is a rejected precedent: a plate under a logo is vetoed. It is named here and not drawn. Motion runs at 200ms on colour, opacity and the appearance of a rule, with no bounce and no scaling, and the Instant row in the console takes the movement away entirely, so nothing is chosen for being pleasant to watch.

The seven candidates and where each comes from
CandidateWhat you seePrecedent, and where it gives out
T1 · mark and a short rule The selected logo takes the state colour, and a short permanent blue rule sits under the mark itself rather than under the whole cell. Kept as a candidate that was tried and lost. Cartier, the selected section inside the open panel. A rule on the mark inherits the accidental width of a brand and reads as a stump under a compact logo such as Amex; at Cartier the trick lives under a line of text, where every label is one face at one size.
T2 · the mark alone No rule at all. The selected logo keeps the state colour and the rest of the row steps back a shade, so the mark carries the choice on its own. Cartier grid and feed, where the pictogram fills instead of gaining a plate; COS, ink against grey. Our logos are one webfont, so full brand colour against monochrome cannot be built literally.
T3 · a rule under the cell The blue rule spans the whole slot, so every mark gets the same geometry whatever its shape. This is the live strip to the pixel, and the candidate this page opens on. Hermes, a rule wider than the label; Four Seasons, a wide marker over a section. Adapted from navigation markers, neither of them a logo tab.
H1 · ink The pointed item deepens to Zinc 900. For that to be visible at all the resting bar sits at Zinc 600, which is a real change to how the bar reads with nobody pointing at it. No weight changes, so no label moves. COS, women and men. A contrast retailer rather than high luxury. Elian's main candidate alongside T1.
H2 · a small chevron Gifts, the one item with a panel behind it, shows a chevron in a place kept for it at rest. Nothing else on the bar answers. Gucci, hover inside the drawer. Carried from a drawer into a top bar, and only for an item that really opens something.
H3 · the panel answers The label stays exactly as it is. The answer is the panel: light, wide, edge to edge under the bar. Items without a panel answer with nothing. La Mer, shop. It works where there is a real mega menu and gives out on a single link.
H4 · a thin dark rule A hairline at the foot of the row, the width of the label, in ink rather than blue. It stays while the panel is open and goes when the pointer leaves. Tiffany, Cartier, Mr Porter: five of the six bars with a confirmed response draw a line. The most direct luxury support there is, and the one that shares geometry with the tabs.
