Drawer

A panel that comes in from the right edge over a dimmed page, and gives the screen back when it leaves. Every drawer in the prototype is this one element with different content in it, and since 28 August every one of them wears the same head. The sections below are in the same order as every other component card, and every number is read off a rendered panel at your current window width.

Window width right now: measuring, and the panel measures measuring at it.

Component

Drawer Stable

One surface, one head, one way out. The page decides what goes on it; nothing about the drawer knows what it is carrying.

Overview

What it is

A fixed panel on the right of the window, sitting over a backdrop that dims the page underneath. It is min(520px, 100%) wide, so on a phone it takes the screen rather than pretending to be a panel on it. One instance per page is enough: opening it a second time swaps the content instead of stacking a second panel, which is why fourteen pages of the prototype carry exactly one <gb-drawer> and everything that needs a surface asks that one for it.

The head is the part that was settled last and matters most. Ton, 28 August: bring every drawer in the house to one denominator. So there is one round slot on the left, the title beside it, and nothing else. No eyebrow, no subtitle, no actions: a Clear all belongs next to the thing it clears, and a sentence about the content opens the body where the content is.

Variants

Four shapes, and there is no fifth

A drawer has no colour axis, no size axis and no type axis: it is one surface, and what varies is which of its optional parts a caller hands over. So the variants are named by what is in the call, and every one of them is standing somewhere in the prototype today.

VariantIn the callWhat it is forWhere it stands today
Without a foottitle, html, and often subThe plain case. Everything the reader needs is in the body, and the body scrolls to the bottom of the panelThe comment thread, and the sign in drawer in the site header
With a footand footA strip that does not scroll with the body: the provenance of what is above it, or the buttons that finish the jobThe properties drawer of Inspect and of every showcase card, and the four drawers of the checkout
With a step backand back, or setBack laterA flow of more than one step. The arrow takes the left slot, the cross moves to the right edge, and Escape still closes rather than steps backThe sign in drawer, from the code step onward: the flow hands the drawer its own way back
With a form insidehtml holding fieldsA drawer is a working surface, not a message. The fields are the field organism, floating labels and all, and the drawer contributes only the gutter around themThe comment thread, the four checkout drawers and the whole sign in flow

Two of these can be worn at once, and three of the four often are: the checkout drawers carry a form and a foot, and the sign in drawer carries a form and grows a step back halfway through. What no drawer carries is a second variant of the head. That was the whole of the 28 August decision, and it is the reason this table has no row for a centred title.

States

Closed, arriving, open, leaving

Four states and two clocks. The panel is dense from the first pixel and travels its own width in 800ms on the entrance curve, while the backdrop dims under it in 280: the light goes down before the panel arrives. Leaving accelerates and is shorter, 350ms for both, the same clock the checkout overlay leaves on. This is a brand signature under Ton-7, so there is no reduced motion branch anywhere in the file: the drawer plays for everyone.

The rows below are measured by toggling the organism's own classes on a real panel and reading the result back, not by copying the stylesheet.

StateWhat is on screenThe clockToken

Escape, a click on the backdrop and the cross all do the same thing, and they do it from any state. A drawer that is already open and is opened again does not play the arrival a second time: the content swaps under a panel that never left, which is what makes a flow of steps feel like one surface rather than four.

Sizes

Every measure, and where each one comes from

A drawer has no size ladder: it is one panel at one width, and the only thing that steps is the gutter and the title, once, at 640. What the table below is really for is the provenance column, because this component sits on three different kinds of number: measured off live, taken from the scales, and a handful that are neither and are named out loud.

One number moves between the two readings and it is worth knowing why before reading the table. An open drawer locks the page behind it, and locking the page takes its scrollbar away, so below 520 the panel is the whole window while it is open and the window less the scrollbar while it is not. Open the drawer above and the row changes under you, with the difference named.

Usage

When a drawer, and when a page

One question decides it before any other: does the reader have to come back to what is underneath? A drawer keeps the context on screen and dimmed behind the panel, and it costs the reader nothing to leave. A page replaces the context, and it is the right answer whenever the work is long enough that being sent back would feel like losing it.

Reach forWhenNot when
A drawerA side task that belongs to something on the page: editing one row, reading the properties of one element, signing in without losing the basketThe main work of a screen. A drawer that is the point of the page is a page in a costume, 520 wide for no reason
A pageWork with steps, work worth a URL, work a reader may want to leave and come back toA confirmation, a detail, a note: sending someone away and back for one field is the more expensive answer
One titleThe head says what the drawer is, once. The body starts with the contentRepeating the title as a heading inside the body. Two names for one surface, eighty pixels apart, is a mistake the reader has to work out
Actions in the bodyNext to the thing they act on: a Clear all at the top of the list it clears, a Save in the foot beside the form it savesIn the head. The head has one control and it is the way out, and that is the whole of the 28 August rule
The step backA flow that genuinely has a step behind it, and only while it does. The flow hands the drawer its own way backAs a second close. Escape and the cross already do that, and an arrow that closes is a lie about where the reader is
A subtitleOne sentence that names what the content is about: whose gift, which element, which shipmentAs an instruction. A sentence the reader has to act on belongs in the body next to the control it is about
A footSomething that must stay visible while the body scrolls: the buttons that finish, or the provenance of what is aboveFor text nobody has to see. An empty foot is better than a full one, and an empty one removes itself

Escape always closes, even when there is a step behind. Escape is the way out of a surface, not a way back through it; the arrow is the way back, and it is a button rather than a gesture because a person has to be able to see that the step exists. And one drawer per page: two panels on top of each other is a stack nobody can leave in one press, which is exactly what the element prevents by swapping its content instead.

Accessibility

Focus, escape and the page underneath

RoleThe panel is role="dialog" with aria-modal="true", and its aria-label is written from the title and the subtitle at the moment it opens, so a reader who cannot see the head is told the same thing it says
Focus inThe panel itself takes focus when it arrives. It is tabindex="-1", so it is reachable by script and not by the tab key, and the first tab from there lands on the first control inside the drawer rather than somewhere on the page behind
Focus outOn close, focus returns to the element that opened it. A reader who pressed a pin, a face or a row goes back to that pin, face or row, not to the top of the document
EscapeCloses, from any step, with a step behind it or without. It is bound while the drawer is open and unbound when it leaves, so it never eats an Escape the page wanted
The backdropA click anywhere on the dimmed page closes the drawer. It is the largest target on the screen and it means the same thing as the cross
The slot namesThe cross is named Close and the arrow is named Back, always. They are glyphs with no label, so without a name a screen reader announces a button and nothing else
The page underneathStops scrolling while the panel is open, and gets its scrolling back on close. Without that, a wheel over the backdrop moves the page the reader is not looking at
The targetThe slot is the 44 circle of the button organism, which is exactly the WCAG 2.2 minimum, and it keeps the organism's focus ring

One honest gap. Focus is moved in and moved back, and the page behind is frozen, but focus is not TRAPPED: a determined tab key can still walk out of the panel and into the page underneath. For a modal dialog that is a real shortcoming rather than a taste, and closing it is a change to the organism, so it is written down here rather than fixed quietly in a showcase wave.

Code

One element, and the call that opens it

One element in the markup, and everything else in the call. The element can be written anywhere on the page: the panel and the backdrop move themselves onto the body when it is built, so a transformed ancestor cannot become their containing block and slide the drawer inside a card.

<!-- once per page, anywhere in the body -->
<gb-drawer id="panel"></gb-drawer>

<link rel="stylesheet" href="system/components/drawer.css">
<script src="system/components/drawer.js"></script>
var panel = document.getElementById('panel');

// the plain case
panel.open({
  title: 'Shipping address',
  sub:   'For Alice Nakamura',
  html:  '<div class="gba-form">...</div>',
  foot:  '<button class="gb-btn gb-btn--m">...</button>'
});

// a flow with a step behind it: the arrow takes the left slot
// and the cross moves to the right edge
panel.open({
  title: 'Enter the code',
  back:  function () { flow.back(); },
  html:  flow.stepHTML()
});

// or grown while the panel is already on screen
panel.setBack(function () { flow.back(); });
panel.setBack(null);
panel.setTitle('Enter the code');
panel.close();

// following it without owning it
panel.addEventListener('gbd:open',  function () { ... });
panel.addEventListener('gbd:close', function () { ... });

Two things a caller does not have to write. There is no close button to add: the head builds its own slots and wires them. And there is no scroll lock, no focus save and no key listener to remember, because a drawer that leaves any of those to its callers has fourteen chances to get one of them wrong.
