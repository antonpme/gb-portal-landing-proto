Design system

Typography

Two families, one root size and a ladder of roles that steps twice as the screen widens. Every role on this page stands as a live specimen, every number under it is read back out of the rendered page at your current window width, and clicking any specimen opens its properties.

Window width right now: measuring, which puts the ladders on the measuring rung. The type ladders step at 640, 768, 1280 and 2000.

Foundation

Overview Stable

Two families and one root. Noto Serif is the title voice and nothing else; Inter carries every other word in the product.

The two families
Title family--font-serif, Noto Serif with Georgia behind it. Titles and title numerals only
Text family--font-sans, Inter with the system stack behind it. Body, leads, captions, labels, fields, tables, everything that is not a title
Root--font-size-root, 16px. The reading size of the product, and the size a paragraph returns to whenever nothing else applies
Thresholds640, 768, 1280 and 2000. The wide rung is the client's own 2xl at 2000 and not the framework's 1536; the widest live figures were read on a 2258 screen and land on that rung
OwnerThe families and the root are tokens in system/assets/tokens.css. The role level is studio/system/TYPE-SCALE.md, and only a few roles have a class yet

One rule decides every disagreement on this page: a measurement beats a drawing. Where the instrument has read a value off the running product, that value is the role, and the design file is a source of names rather than numbers. Where the product has never shown a size, the drawing is used and the row says so out loud. Nothing on this page is interpolated quietly, and nothing is rounded to make a ladder look tidy.

Roles

Scale Counting

The whole ladder, in five groups. Each role gets a specimen you can click and a row of numbers measured off that specimen at this window width. Resize the window and the numbers move with the ladder.

Two kinds of row live in these tables and the difference matters more than any single number. A role with a class is worn by a real stylesheet: the specimen wears the class, the instrument reads what the browser drew, and if the organism ever drifts from the record the page shows it. A role without a class has no stylesheet to read, so its specimen is drawn from the recorded ladder and the instrument reads that back. Those rows are marked, and they are the working list of what the system still owes itself.

Usage

Pairing rules

Which voice, at which size, in which case. Rules only: a role is chosen by what the text does on the screen, never by what some other screen happens to look like.

The voices

The serif is a title voice. It belongs to page titles, section titles and the large numerals that behave as titles. It does not take a paragraph, a caption, a label, a table cell or a form. A serif under a serif under a serif is not a hierarchy, it is a texture: one serif level per screen level, and the level under it is Inter.

Inter carries everything else. Body, leads, captions, labels, fields, tables and buttons. If a piece of text is not the title of the thing it sits on, the family question is already answered.

The floor

Nothing in content goes under 14. Small and faint together is the one combination the owner rejects on sight, so a size at the floor keeps a reading ink and never drops to a pale grey to soften it. Two exceptions exist and both are marks rather than reading: the badge numeral at 10, and the small button label, which the ladder sets at 11 and 12. Neither is a licence for a third.

Case and copy

Caps belongs to exactly two roles: the eyebrow and the button label. Everywhere else the text is sentence case, headings included, and mixed cases inside one screen are a defect rather than a style. The label of a button is written in sentence case in the markup and the organism does the capitalising, so the copy stays readable in the source. No em dashes anywhere: a colon, a comma, a full stop or a middle dot does the job.

The light weight, 300, is the field weight. It is the one place the system reaches for it deliberately. It also appears on a few recorded roles further up this page, and those are records of what the product does rather than an invitation to set the next paragraph in 300.

Hierarchy

The step between two roles has to be visible without measuring it. If a heading and the text under it differ by a pixel or two, that is not a hierarchy, and the fix is to take the heading up a rung rather than to make the body smaller. Weight is a second axis and not a substitute for size: a bolder line at the same size reads as emphasis inside the same level, not as a level above it.

measuring

What the rung switch changes is honest and limited. The title, the lead, the section, the body and the caption have no class in the system, so they are drawn from the recorded ladder and they redraw at whichever rung you pick. The eyebrow and the button are real organisms whose ladders live in a stylesheet, and a stylesheet keys off the window, so those two stay at the rung this window is on. The column width does not move either: only the window can move that, and resizing the window moves everything at once.

Properties

What the drawer reports

Click any specimen on this page and the drawer opens with these seven values, measured off that element, with the token beside each one that has a token.

PropertyWhere the value comes fromToken, when there is one
FamilyThe first family the browser resolved on the element--font-serif or --font-sans
SizeComputed at the current window width, so it names the rung the window is onOnly the roles that have one: the root, the eyebrow, the button label rungs
WeightComputed off the element--eyebrow-weight and the button label weight
Line heightComputed. A role whose line height was never recorded reads as normal, which is the truth about itNone. The system has no line height tokens
TrackingComputed, in pixels, whatever unit the role was recorded in--eyebrow-tracking and the button label tracking
CaseComputed. Only the eyebrow and the button label return uppercaseNone. Case is a property of the role
InkComputed. Most roles record type only and inherit the ink of the surface they stand onThe zinc scale, and the blue of a state

The drawer also names the role, its provenance and its class. Where the row says role without a class yet, the drawer says the same thing and shows the recorded ladder instead of markup to copy, because there is nothing yet to copy. That is the honest state of the system and the reason this page exists in this shape.
