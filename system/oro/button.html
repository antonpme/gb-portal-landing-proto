Variants

One variant at a time, in four sizes and five states

Three types crossed with two colours make the six buttons of a light screen, and inverse makes the ones that stand on a dark one. The table names them all; the stage under it shows one. Pick a variant and the top band walks it up the four size ladders, with the height and the label size read off each specimen, while the band below shows the same variant in five states. Click any specimen to open its properties.

The dark ground is a switch on the stage rather than a second copy of the page, which is how Storybook, Atlassian and Primer all handle a theme. It offers only what belongs on a dark ground: outline and ghost in inverse ink, and the filled primary, which stays exactly as blue on the dark ground as it is on the white one. Contrast measured off the rendered page: white label on Zinc 950 measuring to 1, the blue ground against the plate measuring to 1, which clears the 3 to 1 a control needs, and the white label on that blue measuring to 1.

States

What changes, and to what

The table follows the workbench: it is filled by measuring the five specimens standing above it, so it always describes the variant on the stage and never a general case. Hover always darkens. That is a rule and not a preference: a button that lightens under the pointer reads as switched off, and it is the one thing about our buttons the live product has already corrected us on. Focus is a ring rather than a colour change, so it can sit on top of any other state without arguing with it, and it appears for the keyboard only.

StateGroundInkHairlineToken

The rest specimen on the stage is a real button: hover it, tab to it, hold the pointer down on it. The other four are held still by .is-hover, .is-focus and .is-active, plus a real disabled. Those three classes are demo only. They exist so five states can stand side by side on one screen, and no product page may wear them.

With icon

The glyph climbs the same ladder

The arrow is the live glyph of the site's forward actions. It is drawn in currentColor, so it is always exactly the ink of the label beside it, and its box steps with the size. Which side it stands on is DOM order and nothing else: there is no left modifier and no right modifier, and both at once is two spans.

Size Icon before Icon after Both
S · chromeglyph measuring, label measuring
M · panelsglyph measuring, label measuring
L · formsglyph measuring, label measuring
XL · committingglyph measuring, label measuring

One glyph scale is shared by two ladders on purpose: M reads the L rungs, 16 then 20 then 24, because the two sit one step apart in height and a separate scale for the gap between them would be a number nothing ever measured. XL is the only ladder with a rung of its own, 28 from 2000. In a --block button the glyph leaves the row and parks against the padding edge, which is what the width axis of the playground shows. An icon only button is the --icon shape, added 28.08: a 44px circle holding one glyph and no label, measured off the v1 catalogue drawers and the header. It is a shape rather than a size, because "no label" and "how loud" are separate questions, and it carries an aria-label always: a glyph alone is only legible when its name is available some other way.

The table is measured, not typed: four hidden buttons wearing the four sizes are read with getComputedStyle every time the window changes size, so what you see is what this browser is rendering at this moment. A note on the proportion formula of 17 August, pad-y = 0.24H and pad-x = 0.5H with the gap equal to pad-y. It still describes the small end well: 36 gives 8.6, which rounds to the live 8. Above that, live and the formula part company. The live form submit and the live product CTA both hold pad-y at 8 and run their gaps two or three times wider than that. Where live has measured a number, live wins, and the formula is the tie breaker for the sizes live has never shown, which is why every number of the M ladder is marked declared.

Properties

The modifiers, and the drawer that reads a specimen

Two layers, the way the mature systems keep them. The table is the whole surface of the component, readable and linkable in one piece. The drawer answers a different question, about one specimen: click any button on this page and it opens with the geometry, the type, the colours and the transition measured off that element, with the token that produced each value beside it. It is the first brick of the dev mode Ton asked for, and it means a developer never has to open a stylesheet to learn what a component measures.

Usage

Which cell, and when

Blue is state, black is furniture. That split is not decoration: it is the rule that keeps the accent meaning something, and it decides the colour before anything else does.

The label is copy and follows the copy rules: sentence case in the markup, since the organism sets the caps itself, a verb first, no full stop.

Accessibility

Keyboard, contrast and target size

KeyboardThe focus ring is :focus-visible, so it appears for the person who arrived by Tab and not for a mouse click. Two pixels at two pixels offset, and white instead of blue on a dark ground
Disableddisabled on a button, aria-disabled="true" on a link. The organism disarms both with pointer-events, and the button keeps its colour rather than turning grey so the person can still see which action is waiting on them
IconsEvery glyph is aria-hidden="true" and rides beside a real label. A button with a glyph and no label needs a name of its own, and it needs a tooltip
ContrastThe label on every filled variant clears 4.5 to 1, and the hairline of an outline clears 3 to 1 against its ground. The two dark ground pairs are measured live in the variants section above
Target sizeThe smallest button in the system is 36px tall, and a bar button is 42 from 1280. Nothing in the organism goes under 36
Code

The class, and three words after it

The base class plus three words. Filled, primary and L are the defaults, so a bare gb-btn is already the button most screens want.

<!-- the default: filled, primary, L -->
<button class="gb-btn" type="button">
  <span class="gb-btn__label">Book a meeting</span>
</button>

<!-- a header button: small, outlined, black -->
<a class="gb-btn gb-btn--s gb-btn--outline gb-btn--secondary" href="#">
  <span class="gb-btn__label">My portal</span>
</a>

<!-- the same button on a dark ground -->
<a class="gb-btn gb-btn--s gb-btn--outline gb-btn--inverse" href="#">
  <span class="gb-btn__label">My portal</span>
</a>

<!-- a form submit: full width, glyph at the padding edge -->
<button class="gb-btn gb-btn--l gb-btn--block" type="submit">
  <span class="gb-btn__label">Continue</span>
  <span class="gb-btn__icon"><svg>...</svg></span>
</button>
Changelog

What each old class became

Ton approved the migration on 26 August. Four local button ladders lived across the prototypes, each written for the screen it was needed on, and all four are gone. Nothing was left behind as an alias: an alias is a second name for the same thing, and a second name is the drift this organism exists to end.
