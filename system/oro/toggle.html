Base component

Toggle button Stable

A white track with the chosen half filled black inside it. Two, three or four values, one of them held between them: a toggle does not perform an action, it says which one of a small set is true.

It came off the checkout, where it has asked how a shipment is addressed since 21 August. Everything measured there is the default; the sizes, the glyph and the optical lift of the label were added on 1 September and are marked where they stand.

Window width right now: measuring. The label ladder steps at 640, 1280 and 2000, and the default rung steps only at 2000.

A note on the name, because the client's stack is the client's dictionary and this house got a name wrong once already. Checked on primevue.dev on 1 September: PrimeVue's ToggleButton «is used to select a boolean value using a button», which is one button with two faces. The control on this page, a row of buttons holding one value between them, is their SelectButton, «used to choose single or multiple items from a list using buttons». The house name is still Toggle button. Renaming it would move a class the checkout wears, which is not this wave's to touch, so the finding is recorded and the decision is Ton's.

Playground

Build a toggle and read it back

Set the axes on the rail and the one control on the stage rebuilds itself. It is real: click a half and the value moves, tab to it and the arrows walk the set. Which control an axis gets is not chosen here: GbOro.controls reads the canon of axes, then the count, then what a track would cost against the width of the rail, so Size, Width, Choices and Icon position are toggle buttons, State is a Select and yes or no is a switch.

The specimen wears the screen presets, because they are the one set of choices in this house that already come with glyphs and the icon axis has to have something honest to draw. The control this component was measured off, the checkout's own two words, stands in Anatomy further down.

Sizes

Three rungs, and a height that is not on the grid

The ladder is the button's, one rung short. S is the checkout, measured; M and L spend the button's own M and L pads and labels rather than numbers of their own. There is no XL, and the omission is the argument: XL is the ladder of the one committing action of a template, and a toggle never commits anything. It holds a value while somebody decides.

The track is not on the height grid, and it is not being put there. Every other control in the house stands on 72 / 64 / 56 / 48 / 40 / 36 because it declares a height. This one declares none: the track is its label plus its air, so it comes out where the arithmetic leaves it: the three numbers measured above, none of them a rung. Pinning it is the one change the component cannot take. At 390 the checkout's second label wraps to two lines inside its own half and the track grows with it; a fixed height would clip exactly that, which is the bug gbppl-checkout-15 fixed on this very band. So the size axis moves the label and the air, and the height follows. The one rung it does touch is a coincidence worth knowing: at 1280 the default track measures within half a pixel of 42, the live height of a header button, and nothing about the component put it there.

The vertical pad is a flat 8 on all three rungs, which is not laziness but the button family's own behaviour: pad-y is 8 on S, M, L and XL alike, measured. What the rungs move is the horizontal pad and the label. And the S rung is the one that does not climb at 1280. Its 8 by 16 was read on the checkout at three widths and live wins over the family, so that step is a missing line in the stylesheet rather than an override on top of one.

With icon

A mark beside the word, on either side of it

A glyph in a half is .gb-icon --16 out of the icon record, not a slot of this component: a mark that does not answer the pointer is an icon, and the half itself is the control. Which side it stands on is DOM order and nothing else: there is no left modifier and no right modifier, exactly as on the button, and both at once is two spans. The air between mark and word is --space-8.

The glyph is 16 on every rung and at every width, and that is the button family's own pairing rather than a number picked here: the button's S ladder carries a flat 16 beside its 11px label at all three widths, and its L ladder pairs the same 16 with a 14px label at base. So a mark in a toggle half never disagrees with a mark in a button standing beside it. Beside a word it also costs nothing in height at the S rung, because the 16 box is shorter than the 16.5 line box of the label and the word still sets the height. A half with a mark and no word is half a pixel shorter, which the caption above measures rather than hides.

A half with a mark and no word is legal and drawn above, and it needs an aria-label of its own, the same contract the icon-only button makes. It is not a modifier and there is no --icon shape here: on the button that shape took a wave and a live measurement to earn, and a toggle of bare marks has no carrier in this product. If one appears, it arrives with a measurement.

States

What changes, and to what

The pressed half is not a state, it is the value: on a toggle it is always on screen, which is the whole difference from a button. So the states below belong to the half that is not chosen, the one a pointer and a keyboard can still reach. The table is filled by measuring the specimens standing above it.

Hover darkens, and it darkens twice: the wash comes up and the ink steps from Zinc 700 to Zinc 900. That pair replaced a measured Zinc 50 on 1 September, because five units of grey against a white track is a hover nobody sees and Ton said so out loud; the wash it took instead is the one the ghost button twelve pixels away in the same checkout band already spends. The pressed half is never washed: it is already the answer, and a black card going grey under the pointer would read as a fault.

There is no press state, and that is a decision rather than an omission. A button needs one because pushing it produces nothing you can see until it has finished; pushing a half of a toggle turns it black within the same 160 milliseconds, so the press and its result are the same paint. Adding one would mean inventing a colour the checkout never rendered in order to show it for a sixth of a second.

Hover and focus above are held still by .is-hover and .is-focus, the two demo names the button already declares. They exist so five states can stand on one screen and no product page may wear them. The last two are real: a native disabled on one half, and .is-disabled with aria-disabled on the whole group.

Width

Hug, block or equal shares

Three answers to one question, and only the first is the checkout. A track that hugs is as wide as its words; --block spans its column and lets the halves fall to a second row inside it; --fill spans the column and splits it in equal shares, one label centred in each. The last two are alternatives, never a pair.

Hug no modifier, the default, and the checkout measuring
Block --block, the track spans and the halves keep their own widths measuring
Fill --fill, the track spans and the halves split it in equal shares measuring

Under --fill one measured number changes, and it changes because it stops meaning what it meant: the horizontal pad drops to --space-8 on every rung. In a hug the 16 is the half's air. In a share the air is whatever the share leaves over, and the padding is only the floor between a centred label and its own edge, so it is a floor and not a ladder. Nothing visible moves: a filled half is as wide as its share and never as wide as its padding.

The one pixel of uneven padding, and why it is there. Ton, on 1 September: the text inside the toggle looks pushed up. It was, and by nearly a whole pixel. The pressed half was shot at eight device pixels to the css pixel, with the card's own edges and the white ink found in the same image so that no rounding of a crop could be mistaken for the effect, and that put the ink above the optical centre. The cause is the alphabet, not the stylesheet: a line box is balanced around the font's ascent and descent, and an all-caps label has no descender to put in the space reserved under the baseline, so the ink floats up by half of it. The correction is one device pixel taken off the top pad and given to the bottom one. The sum is untouched, so the half, the track and every rectangle on the checkout stand exactly where they stood: measured after the change. Declared under Ton-17, because the source rendered it high and an obvious shortfall of the source is repaired at the owner.

What the button has and this does not, each for a reason written at the rule that would have carried it. No colour family: the checkout's own comment settles it, «black, not blue: the toggle is furniture, not a state chip», and a blue toggle would make the accent mean «chosen» when it already means «the action being asked for» (Ton-5). No type: filled, outline and ghost describe how loud an action is, and a toggle's whole paint is one half against the other. No XL, for the reason under Sizes. No text field for the label and no picker for the glyph, the two axes the button's own rail carries: a button has one word and one mark, so a control can edit them; a toggle has one of each per value, and a control that edits a set is a table.

Usage

When a toggle, and when something else

DoDo not
Reach for a toggle when the whole set is short and the reader benefits from seeing all of it at onceUse one past four values. Five is a Select: a track that long stops being comparable and starts being a list
Keep the labels short and of one kind, so the halves read as a set rather than as separate buttonsMix a verb with a noun. A half is a value, not an action, and a half that says «Save» is a button in the wrong clothes
Always leave one half chosen. A toggle has an answer from the moment it is drawnDraw one with nothing pressed and treat the empty state as a question. That is what a form field with a placeholder is for
Say what is being chosen, in a heading beside the track or an aria-label on itLeave a bare pair of words in a band and trust the paragraph under it to explain them
Let the host own the value when choosing does more than store a string: opens half a form, reloads a listWire a host handler and leave the component driving as well. Two owners answer twice
Use --fill when the track sits in a column of its own and a ragged right edge would read as a mistakeUse --fill for labels that will not fit their share. A share that has to wrap is a schema that should not have asked for a toggle

Two segments in this house look alike and are not the same control. This one is a value of a form, black and boxed, and it belongs on a product screen. .gbdoc-seg in docs.css is quiet text with a blue underline, it switches the view of a document, and it exists because an instrument must not look like the thing it builds (Ton, 26.08). Whether they should be one component with two looks is an open question in the skill; until it is answered, pick by what the control does, not by how it looks.

The labels are copy and follow the copy rules: sentence case in the markup, since the component sets the caps itself, and no full stop.

Accessibility

One tab stop, and the arrows do the choosing

GroupingThe track is role="radiogroup" with a name and each half is a role="radio", set by toggle.js where the markup has not set them. A group written with aria-pressed instead is left as it is: two pressed buttons are a legal reading of the same control, and a second attribute beside the first would be a second source of truth
KeyboardOne tab stop for the whole group, on the chosen half. The arrows move between the halves and choose as they go, Home and End jump to the ends, and all of them wrap. That is the pattern a browser gives a radio group everywhere else, so nobody has to learn this one
Why the arrows also pressThey fire a real click, which means the host's own handler runs. A host-driven group gets keyboard support without this component knowing anything about what the host does
Focus:focus-visible, so the ring appears for the person who arrived by Tab and not for a mouse click. Two pixels of Blue 600 at two pixels of offset, the button organism's own ring. Declared: the checkout showed the browser's hairline and nothing designed
DisabledA real disabled on a half takes it out of the tab order and out of the arrow walk. The whole group off wants aria-disabled on the track and disabled on the halves, because pointer-events cannot take a button out of the tab order
GlyphsEvery mark is aria-hidden and rides beside a real word. A half with a mark and no word carries its own aria-label
Target sizeThe default half is 32.5 tall inside a 42.5 track, which is under the 44 a thumb wants and is what live measures. Where a toggle is the subject of a touch screen rather than a band of a wide form, the M and L rungs are there
A picture of a controlA track inside aria-hidden is a preview, not a control: toggle.js leaves it entirely alone, so a registry tile has no tab stop and announces nothing
Code

A track, and a button for every value

The word is a bare text node when it stands alone and a .gb-toggle__label when a mark stands beside it. Both are legal, and the checkout writes the first.

<!-- the component owns the value -->
<div class="gb-toggle" role="radiogroup" aria-label="Screen">
  <button class="gb-toggle__item is-on" type="button"
          role="radio" aria-checked="true" data-value="desktop">Desktop</button>
  <button class="gb-toggle__item" type="button"
          role="radio" aria-checked="false" data-value="mobile">Mobile</button>
</div>

<!-- a mark beside the word: .gb-icon, and the word in its own span -->
<button class="gb-toggle__item" type="button" role="radio" aria-checked="false">
  <span class="gb-icon gb-icon--16" aria-hidden="true"><svg>...</svg></span>
  <span class="gb-toggle__label">Mobile</span>
</button>

<!-- equal shares in a column of its own, one rung up -->
<div class="gb-toggle gb-toggle--m gb-toggle--fill" role="radiogroup" aria-label="Screen">
  ...
</div>

<!-- the host owns the value: group, tab stop and arrow keys only -->
<div class="gb-toggle" data-gb-toggle="host" role="radiogroup" aria-label="Addresses">
  ...
</div>

Link toggle.css after button.css, because every rung of the label ladder and both state values are the button's own tokens, and icon.css as well if any half carries a mark. toggle.js runs last and picks up tracks that arrive later: a shipment card rendered by a template and a rail rebuilt by a panel are both enhanced the moment they land.
